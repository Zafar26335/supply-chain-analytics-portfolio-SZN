Company-Calibrated Synthetic Dataset for Academic Analysis — illustrative figures, calibrated to Lucky Cement's disclosed scale, not actual company records.

SCOPE AND ASSUMPTION LOG
One illustrative domestic Karachi/Sindh dealer territory selling Ordinary Portland Cement is analysed in tonnes per week. A starting level of 1,550 tonnes and a modest 1.2-tonne-per-week underlying trend represent a small dealer territory rather than company-wide growth. Broad March-June construction strength adds 240 tonnes, while an assumed July-September monsoon block suppresses sales; these are teaching assumptions, not measured regional seasonality. Prices move in four steps from PKR 20,000 to PKR 22,800 per tonne, with a modest imperfect negative association; dealer discounts of 2%-5% occur in 34 weeks and produce approximately 14%-26% dispatch uplift, potentially including forward buying. A synthetic construction-activity index is chosen because project activity plausibly affects cement orders, and independent noise prevents a perfect relationship. Four one-off shocks and five stockout weeks create abnormal observed sales, while Gaussian noise of about 105 tonnes adds weekly variation. The data do not capture actual Lucky Cement sales, dealer credit, final-user consumption, brand mix, government project timing or true unconstrained demand.

Reconciliation in PKR thousands: 192,861,184 gross revenue - 53,227,737 sales tax/FED - 3,106,430 rebates = 136,527,017 net revenue. Mirrored extracted statement rows were shifted; the company financial-performance summary and arithmetic were used, not corrupted row labels. FY2026 synthetic territory net-sales proxy: PKR 2.021 billion, 1.48% of standalone revenue. This is a scale sanity check, not a verified territory share. Group revenue is not used to calibrate a single cement territory. The latest annual report is recorded by PSX but its PDF could not be fetched in this environment; the financial results summary is the accessible calibration evidence.

OPERATIONAL ASSUMPTIONS
Weekly territory dispatch capacity: 2,400 tonnes. Base-volume commitment: 80%, spread across the horizon; flexible balance: 20%, adjusted weekly. Flexible supply assumed available with one-week response. Assumed variable inventory cost: PKR 17,000/tonne; assumed lost contribution: PKR 4,500/tonne. These are independent teaching inputs, not audited company unit economics. Buffer: twice holdout MAD, not statistically optimised safety stock. Opening stock is zero apart from this separately procured buffer. No storage/financing cost, indirect taxes, demand backlog recovery or late dispatch catch-up is modelled. Stocks remain usable within the horizon by assumption; cement storage/deterioration would require a separate operational review. Lost sales are not recovered in later weeks in the simulation.

METHOD AND MONITORING ASSUMPTIONS
SES alpha fixed at 0.3, MA window four weeks, seasonal naive lag 52 weeks. Seasonal-trend model uses a linear time term and monthly dummies. No specifications changed after holdout results. The +/-4 Tracking Signal threshold and 10% weekly review trigger are assumed policy settings. Future +/-2 MAD bands are illustrative risk bands, not 95% intervals. All models use training targets only; diagnostic regression uses historical training driver values and is not presented as a validated causal forecast.
